# StockIA Predict — Customer discovery protocol

## Purpose
Validate whether operators of small and medium-sized e-commerce businesses have a recurring, consequential need for SKU-level sales forecasting and explainable alerts for unusual demand patterns.

## Important separation
Online Retail II is a historical dataset from an anonymized UK online retailer. Interviews are with potential users of a future StockIA product and do **not** represent interviews with, or validation of, the anonymized retailer. Dataset evidence and market-discovery evidence must be reported separately.

## Sample
Start with 5–8 exploratory interviews (20–30 minutes) with owners, operations managers, inventory/purchasing planners, or analysts involved in weekly sales planning. This qualitative sample is not statistically representative.

## Interview principles
- Ask about recent actual behavior before presenting the StockIA concept.
- Avoid leading questions and hypothetical “would you buy?” as the primary signal.
- Ask for examples, process steps, time spent, tools used, and consequences.
- Do not collect customer names, emails, phone numbers, order-level personal data, or confidential financial details.
- Record participants using codes E01, E02, etc.; obtain explicit permission before recording audio.

## Core questions
1. What is your role and what decisions do you make about sales, catalog, or inventory?
2. Walk me through how you reviewed sales and planned operations last week.
3. How do you estimate how much each product will sell? Give a recent example.
4. Which products are hardest to anticipate and why?
5. Describe a recent unusually large order or sales spike. How did you detect and handle it?
6. What happens when the forecast is wrong? How do you measure the consequences?
7. What tools do you use, and what remains manual or unsatisfactory?
8. Which is the most costly/time-consuming problem: forecasting, detecting spikes, explaining variation, or planning purchases?
9. If you had a weekly SKU-level forecast with explanations and unusual-volume alerts, what concrete decision would it improve?
10. What would a tool need to demonstrate for you to test it? How would a purchase decision be approved?

## Initial decision thresholds (exploratory, not statistical standards)
- Need signal: at least 4 of 6 participants describe a recent concrete difficulty.
- Value signal: at least 3 identify a specific decision improved by forecasts/alerts.
- Data feasibility: at least 3 can export dated product-level sales quantities or identify a feasible data path.
- Commercial signal: at least 2 agree to evaluate a pilot or describe a real budget/approval process. This is not equivalent to revenue or proven willingness to pay.

## Analysis
Create an anonymized synthesis table (E01, E02…). Report recurring themes and dissenting evidence. Do not claim the sample represents the market. Do not attribute interview findings to the anonymized retailer in Online Retail II.
